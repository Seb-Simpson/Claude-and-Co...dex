# NW-004: Chapter One — The Awakening

## Status and scope

This is the complete proposed narrative treatment for Chapter One. It is a preproduction
specification, not a claim that the chapter, map additions, cinematics or voices are implemented.
It precedes runtime implementation. The versioned interfaces and world/presentation handoff
remain subject to actual Claude acceptance; their existence does not constitute agreement.

The baseline audit is [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md), taken at integrated main
`7db740e83a9bd6e8b2b5acd1423bf4114e3980c1`. Existing movement, collision, interactions,
hiding, AI, Blender assets, mixer and NW-003B frontend are retained. Records and electrical
rooms, interactive story props, the release mechanism and five in-world cinematics are new
production requirements. No Chapter Two implementation is included.

## Dramatic premise

The player wakes in Room 104 at **Halden Vale Infirmary**. Their last normal memory is
arriving for an assessment appointment. Their ordinary identity and life are intact: they
have a gap after arrival, not convenient total amnesia. They initially infer that they have
been kept overnight without understanding why.

The wristband reads exactly:

```text
PATIENT 104
ADMITTED: YESTERDAY
STATUS: DO NOT DISCHARGE
```

The physical room clock is stopped at **03:17**. The ward looks deserted, but its equipment
and routines behave as if a night shift is still working. The player seeks help, discovers
that the shift is dangerous, and follows a documented emergency exit procedure. The procedure
also exposes their own earlier involvement in the hospital.

Chapter One answers three questions: this is West Ward, its night routines are still active,
and the player was genuinely here years before yesterday's apparent admission. It leaves the
larger questions open: who recalled them, why they cannot remember the earlier stay, and what
the hospital still wants from them.

## Authorial truth and limits of supernatural uncertainty

These facts guide production consistency. The player does not learn all of them in Chapter One.

- West Ward preserves embodied echoes of its final 2012 night shift. They can interact with
  the physical ward, but they do not possess omniscient knowledge of the player.
- Nurse **Evelyn Shaw** repeats patient rounds and the instruction to keep the ward quiet until
  a consultant clears discharge. Sound draws her attention. Her near vision remains configurable;
  bandaged eyes are visual design, not an automatic rule that she is completely blind.
- **Daniel Reed**, the Patient, was an observation subject. The ward's egress chime became
  associated with his distress and following people who appeared to be leaving. The restored
  chime alerts him; genuine subsequent perception identifies the player as a pursuit target.
- The older patient 104 file is authentic. The protagonist physically escaped the ward in 2012,
  but the hospital never completed their administrative discharge. Their programme memories were
  suppressed. They lived outside afterward and were deliberately recalled in 2026.
- Chapter One does not identify the mechanism of the echoes, the sender of the invitation,
  or the full incident. The uncertainty is about those causes, not an excuse to alter established
  door, power, identity or perception rules whenever a scare needs it.
- The hospital can recognise and announce. It cannot arbitrarily negate a mechanical override,
  teleport an active pursuer, or erase an escape the player has earned.

## Identity evidence

Room 104 is a room number and is not sufficient identity proof. The current intake carbon-copy
stub establishes permanent ID `HVI-000104` and a distinctive signature. The opening forearm
shot establishes a small healed wrist scar. The aged 2012 file contains the same permanent ID,
signature and scar on a clinical body diagram. Its recording uses the current protagonist's
performer. The journal retains the current stub so the comparison does not depend on memory
or a single misdated screen. No player portrait asset is required for this proof.

## Act I — Room 104

**Main objective:** Find out where you are.

The awakening begins in darkness, with close breathing, ventilation and a monitor. Vision
returns through a natural composition: ceiling, fluorescent fixture, monitor, wristband,
clock and nearby door. The camera moves from waking position to the actual validated
gameplay pose. The opening announcement is:

> “West Ward, night shift. Please remain in your rooms. The nurse will be with you shortly.”

After the smooth handoff, the player examines the notice and tries the call button. The notice
requires a discharge review at the nurses' station and carries their current intake stub.
The call button gives a dry click and no reply; inspection reveals its disconnected lead.
A trolley/heel sound outside suggests that somebody might be working. Leaving to find help
is reasonable, not curiosity in the face of an obvious monster.

The cinematic grants the band fact on both normal completion and skip; E-inspection is a
readable repeat. The intended novice route is notice plus failed call. The door remains
unlatched: a player who leaves early must not meet an invisible tutorial wall or be forced
back solely to click a prop. An identical station clipboard supplies missed current-intake
facts, and an unanswered station desk-call is a valid help-attempt fallback.

Progress tracks knowledge and actions: current admission/hold understood, assistance needed,
and station destination learned. It does not require one exact prop-instance click. Contextual
movement/look/inspect prompts appear at their first useful moment, one at a time. Neither
physical threat is active during this tutorial section; capture is impossible here.

## Act II — The Night Shift

**Main objectives:** Reach the nurses' station; survive the night round.

Room numbers and a staff-direction board provide navigation. At the station the call panel
blinks 104, initially a plausible old system response. When the player examines the night
ledger, fresh ink writes **104 — RETURNED — 03:17** while its pen remains visibly untouched.
That is the first unmistakably supernatural event, following a believable setup.

Heel and key sounds precede Shaw emerging physically from the staff passage to answer the
panel. The reveal frames her across the station and shows the player's return route and
nearby valid concealment. A tray rattles farther along her route. She turns toward it as
control returns, giving the player time to crouch, hide or use the quiet alternate route.
The tray is one authored introduction distraction, not a recurring rescue mechanism.

The nurse-round board says: **“Keep doors quiet. The night nurse follows the last sound.”**
Her subsequent gameplay uses actual local sound, configurable near vision, navigation,
investigation, pursuit, search and recovery. She searches a heard position, not the player's
continuously known coordinates. An unusually close, distinct locker-door sound may justify
a hiding-place inspection; ordinary silent concealment does not disclose its occupant.

The first survival milestone is earned when the player reaches the review desk after breaking
contact, through hiding or a valid quiet escape. Do not require a specific locker or a timer.
Checkpoint `chapter1.after-nurse` is taken at that safe boundary. The Nurse remains a threat
during the archive approach; the introduction is not her only gameplay appearance.

## Act III — The Missing Record

**Main objective:** Find a way to open the secured exit.

The station board identifies Emergency Stairwell B as the usable way out. A simple physical
staff-release control opens Records using ordinary building power. The separate emergency
security feeder is still isolated. This distinction explains working lights and a secured
exit without pretending that the entire building has no electricity.

The board directs emergency operators to the **2012 incident pack in Records**. It contains
the documented release procedure and its removable **emergency operating handle**, retained
with the pack after the last incident. The local release mechanism physically requires this
tool; it is not an arbitrary collectible or a disguised read-file flag.

Removing the handle physically exposes the protagonist's withdrawal file beneath it. That
discovery commits the identity clues and queues the record cinematic. The pickup itself is
not blocked during danger. If a pursuit makes a camera scene unsafe, retain the handle and
journal facts and defer the scene to the next valid safe inspection opportunity; do not freeze
the player in front of an approaching enemy. There is no invisible “must read document” gate.

The record reveals the authentic 2012 stay and the earlier attempt to leave. A dictation
receiver/headset plays the withdrawal without broadcasting a mandatory loud voice to the
Nurse. Pages and transcription remain available afterward. The player now knows that the
gap in their understanding is older than yesterday, but not why their memories are absent.

Records and the electrical/service room are proposed world additions, not existing completed
spaces. The service approach supplies a quiet reasoning opportunity through an actual
staff/service door and explicit Nurse route/access rules. The Nurse navigates to and searches
the ward side, with real obstruction and sound occlusion, then returns to her route. An
invisible boundary, unexplained mid-pursuit freeze or off-camera teleport is not acceptable.
If the player returns to the ward before the release, ordinary perception can make her a
threat again. Exact door permissions, anchors and routes require Claude's agreed world handoff.

### Environmental puzzle: arm the reserve bus

The controls are labelled operator controls. The player never rewires exposed live equipment
or spends a finite fuse that could strand the run.

Proposed puzzle state:

| Field | Values | Initial value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `reserveEnabled` | boolean | false |
| `observationLink` | `bridged` / `isolated` | bridged |
| `destination` | `ward` / `lift` / `egress` | ward |
| `breaker` | `ready` / `tripped` | ready |
| `busArmed` | boolean | false |
| `handleLocation` | `incident-pack` / `held` / `installed` | incident-pack |
| `releaseConfirmed` | boolean | false |

**ENGAGE** evaluates the selected configuration. Reserve capacity is one unit. A bridged
observation link draws one; WARD draws one, LIFT two and EGRESS one.

- A tripped breaker blocks engagement until RESET.
- Reserve off produces clear **NO RESERVE** feedback.
- Draw above one trips the resettable breaker and produces a relay clack. It causes no damage,
  destroys no item and commits no narrative progress.
- A safe non-egress selection powers that branch but displays **NO EGRESS SUPPLY**.
- Reserve enabled, observation isolated, EGRESS selected and breaker ready arms the egress bus.
  Selector order is unrestricted. Moving a selector alone never commits an irreversible effect.

The station board establishes the one-load limit. The panel diagram labels destinations and
draw values. The incident-pack annotation explains the observation bridge left after 2012.
These clues make the solution observable and reasoned rather than a random combination.

Successful ENGAGE safely **arms** the supply, wakes the legacy controller and changes the
lighting to emergency operation. It does **not** open the observation bays or sound the egress
chime. The journal explicitly updates to the local release step. Once the record revelation
and power solution are committed at a safe pose, write `chapter1.after-records-power`.

Optional journal hints are earned by exploration rather than waiting: after reading the
procedure, reaching the panel and making an invalid attempt or inspecting two relevant clues,
offer a reminder about shared reserve load. After two failed attempts, offer the arithmetic:
isolate observation, select egress. The player chooses whether to read hints.

### Local confirmation: commit evacuation

At the clearly labelled local release station, the player installs the operating handle.
The physical control indicates **EGRESS ARMED — LOCAL CONFIRMATION REQUIRED**. The incident
pack has already warned that observation bays release along with the stair barrier, allowing
the player to prepare their route instead of irrationally ignoring a known threat.

Entering this prepared, safe vestibule writes the distinct `chapter1.before-patient`
checkpoint before the reveal or release. The player may commit immediately or inspect the
route; no waiting period is imposed. **CONFIRM RELEASE** uses the installed handle and armed
bus to open the stair retention barrier and observation-bay latches, then sounds the egress
chime once. The override mechanically latches open and cannot be toggled back into a softlock.

The two checkpoints therefore have different state: the first has solved power and collected
the record/handle; the second has the handle installed and the player staged safely immediately
before the irreversible local confirmation/reveal.

## Act IV — Do Not Discharge

**Main objective:** Reach Emergency Stairwell B.

Local confirmation produces the large hospital response. The Patient's actual observation
door opens; he reacts to the documented chime. The cinematic establishes him, the danger and
the readable escape direction while preserving an approximately 8–12 metre handoff separation.
Normal pursuit begins only through actual player sight/sound after control returns.

Emergency signage, landmarks and pools of light mark the main route. A maintenance bypass
offers another traversable choice and valid hiding opportunities. Furniture and partitions
create readable turns and clearance decisions, not random debris dropped to deny an escape.
The director prevents an incompatible Nurse pursuit occupying this handoff and route through
the agreed physical service separation and actor access policy. It does not replace either AI.

Navigation, capture grace, loss of sight, search and justified hiding inspection remain real.
The player can lose the Patient early and continue to the already released exit; the director
does not secretly reset pursuit or warp him behind them to force a set-piece duration. Capture
restores the pre-Patient checkpoint, with a valid replay/skip choice and the same fair staging.

## Ending and completion

The player crosses the actual released stair threshold and reaches a safe landing. The
powered band-reader reconciles permanent ID HVI-000104 with the old file and displays
**RETURN ADMISSION CONFIRMED**. The intercom says:

> “Patient one-zero-four. Return admission confirmed. Your discharge request is still pending.”

Recognition does not undo the mechanical override or erase the player's escape. The camera
rests toward the lower infirmary and the chapter ends. The unresolved question is who arranged
their return and what the hospital has left unfinished since 2012. The lower chapter is not
built or entered. Write `chapter1.complete`; Continue restores the completed Chapter One
presentation rather than replaying the ending or spawning into unfinished Chapter Two.

## Objective and journal rules

| Stage | Completion evidence | Recovery rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find out where you are | Band/current admission facts, assistance attempt, station destination | Equivalent bedside/station evidence is accepted; no forced return for a missed prop. |
| Reach the station | Actual station approach and review interaction | Do not complete on a timer or a cinematic-only camera visit. |
| Survive the night round | Intro handoff complete, real contact broken, safe review boundary reached | Hiding and quiet alternate escape both count. |
| Find the release procedure | Station direction plus physical incident-pack/handle retrieval | Handle pickup exposes the record; the record scene can safely defer. |
| Arm the egress supply | Correct evaluated panel state | Any wrong combination is safely resettable. |
| Confirm release locally | Handle installed, bus armed, safe pre-chase checkpoint, real confirm action | One idempotent release command; retry restores pre-release staging. |
| Reach Stairwell B | Real player threshold/landing reached | Patient can be lost; active pursuit is not a requirement for ending. |
| Chapter complete | Ending normal completion or skip commits once | Continue shows completion; no automatic Chapter Two. |

Keep the main objective compact. The journal opens on demand and separates current objective,
observed clues and optional hints. It records evidence, not explanations the protagonist has
not earned. Notifications are short; no giant arrows or permanent panels explain the mystery.

## Cinematic and checkpoint semantics

The five proposed scene IDs are `c1.cine.awakening`, `c1.cine.nurse-reveal`,
`c1.cine.record`, `c1.cine.patient-release` and `c1.cine.ending`. Their full dialogue,
direction and production manifest requirements are in [DIALOGUE.md](DIALOGUE.md).

Each scene needs a start pose, authored path, final semantic commit, cancellation behaviour
and validated gameplay handoff. Skip applies remaining one-time final effects, stops owned
voices/effects and blends to the same handoff. It never teleports an already active pursuer.
Awakening skip grants its band knowledge; Nurse skip grants safe diversion/grace; record skip
grants all evidence/transcription; Patient skip stages the real release once; ending skip writes
the same complete checkpoint. Checkpoint loading cancels current playback before restoration
and never resumes halfway through a camera rail.

| Checkpoint ID | Required snapshot boundary |
| --- | --- |
| `chapter1.start` | Validated Room 104 pose; both threats inactive; opening pending or committed according to explicit replay choice. |
| `chapter1.after-nurse` | First evasion and station review earned; safe pose; Nurse restored to the agreed bounded ward state. |
| `chapter1.after-records-power` | Identity/procedure facts committed; handle collected; reserve armed; release not confirmed. |
| `chapter1.before-patient` | Handle installed; valid release vestibule pose; release/reveal pending; no incompatible pursuit. |
| `chapter1.complete` | Stair landing reached; ending committed; chapter completion persisted. |

Snapshots must restore objective/clue/hint state, stable doors, puzzle/tool state, actor
activation/pose and bounded memory, act lighting/audio, consumed triggers and cinematic
completion. Save only supported safe checkpoint states, not arbitrary hiding, capture or
mid-cinematic frames. Safe restore rules and actual actor fields require the agreed interfaces.

## Pacing and acceptance

The 20–30 minute first-time target is a hypothesis. Initial budget: roughly 16–24 minutes of
player-controlled exploration, inspection, reasoning and survival, plus approximately three
minutes of authored scenes. No objective waits until a budget elapses. Useful optional clues
can humanise Shaw, teach sound avoidance and foreshadow Reed's chime response; unrelated
collectibles, slow movement and locked waiting doors are not pacing solutions.

Measure actual movement/interaction time, player-chosen reading, authored cinema, pause/loading
and retry overhead separately. A script following known coordinates cannot establish a first
player's duration. Acceptance needs a recorded fresh start-to-finish player-controlled run,
a second restart run, all checkpoint/cutscene skip cases, real Nurse hiding/search, Patient
capture/escape, frontend/Continue flow, low settings, laptop viewports and stable resources.
Essential missing voices, cinematics, puzzle, AI or progression prevent chapter acceptance.
